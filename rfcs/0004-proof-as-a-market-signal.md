# RFC 0004 — Proof as a Market Signal

**Status:** Proposed  
**Date:** 2026-08-29  
**System:** Our Lovely System — Pledge  
**Authors:** Will Daly; Codex as recorder, drafting assistant, and in-character hostile reviewer  
**Related:** [RFC 0001 — Capital Disposition Voting Test](./0001-capital-disposition-voting-test.md), [RFC 0003 — Ours to Give](./0003-ours-to-give.md)  
**Supersedes:** Nothing

## Summary

This RFC proposes a governing premise for features that exist to prove Our Lovely System:

> **By proving Our Lovely System, we add value to Our Lovely System.**

The outcome of proof is not the statement:

> “We have proved Our Lovely System.”

Our Lovely System as a whole is not awarded a certificate of completion. A bounded claim is tested under declared conditions. The test produces evidence and consequence. That evidence enters a human market of judgment.

> **Proof is a market signal.**

People decide what they are willing to risk, lend, contribute, accept, honor, challenge, or carry after encountering the evidence.

## Governing claims

> **The outcome of proving Our Lovely System is a system that has borne a consequence it previously only described.**

> **Proof is not the claim that the system worked. Proof is what happened because the claim was tested.**

> **Proof does not announce value. Proof enters the market and discovers whether value exists.**

## What proof means

Proof in this RFC is bounded and evidentiary.

Examples include:

- this vote governed this capital under this published rule;
- this receipt reconstructed this contribution;
- this deletion request followed this advertised process;
- this user exercised this power and produced this consequence;
- this failure remained visible;
- this correction changed the system; or
- this accepted OTG produced this honored capital.

None of these proves Our Lovely System in total. Each supplies evidence about what a particular part of the system could bear under particular conditions.

## Proof cycle

The proposed cycle is:

1. **Claim** — Pledge describes a bounded capability or rule.
2. **Test** — someone exercises it under declared conditions.
3. **Consequence** — something happens that matters beyond the interface.
4. **Evidence** — the test leaves an inspectable artifact, result, failure, receipt, transfer, correction, or scar.
5. **Signal** — people encounter that evidence.
6. **Response** — people alter what they are willing to risk, lend, contribute, accept, honor, challenge, or carry.
7. **Value adjustment** — the system gains or loses value according to human response.

The system does not control the final judgment merely because it produced or hosted the test.

## Proof object

A proof record should identify:

- the bounded claim;
- claim and rule version;
- test conditions;
- participating actors and attribution posture;
- thing placed at risk or under control;
- expected consequence;
- observed consequence;
- evidence produced;
- known limitations;
- failures and corrections;
- who performed verification; and
- what remains unproved.

A declaration of success without this supporting structure is not sufficient proof.

## Market signal

Proof changes the information available to people considering participation.

Market response may appear as:

- lending a voice;
- lending an identity;
- creating an OTG pledge;
- giving an OTG a **Yeah, okay**;
- accepting or honoring an OTG;
- participating in a vote;
- relying upon a receipt;
- challenging a result;
- exploiting an advertised vulnerability;
- declining to participate; or
- leaving.

The market in this RFC is not limited to money. It includes human judgment expressed through attention, avoidance, participation, responsibility, contribution, dissent, and exit.

Silence must remain silence. Lack of response does not prove agreement, rejection, understanding, or indifference.

## Properties of a strong proof signal

A proof signal becomes stronger when it is:

- bounded to a specific claim;
- connected to a real consequence;
- difficult to imitate cheaply;
- inspectable;
- attributable where attribution is relevant;
- reproducible where reproduction is possible;
- independently challengeable;
- explicit about uncertainty;
- preserved with dissent and correction; and
- honest about what remains unproved.

Cost alone does not establish truth. A costly spectacle can still be false, irrelevant, or manipulative.

## Success, failure, and correction

A successful proof adds evidence of demonstrated capability.

A failed proof adds evidence of demonstrated limitation when the failure remains inspectable.

A corrected failure adds evidence that the system can respond to what it could not previously bear.

A concealed failure subtracts value while manufacturing appearance.

The proposed compact rule is:

> **Proof creates value.  
> Failure creates evidence.  
> Correction preserves value.  
> Concealment destroys it.**

This rule does not guarantee that every test increases market value. Honest evidence may reduce participation or expose that a feature should not survive. The value added may be epistemic: people can make better decisions because the system became more knowable.

## No total proof

Pledge must not label Our Lovely System globally `proved`.

The system changes, its users change, the environment changes, and every proof has conditions and limits. A passed test remains evidence of what happened, not permanent immunity from future failure.

Proof records should therefore say:

> This claim was tested under these conditions and produced this result.

They should not say:

> Our Lovely System has been proved.

## No sole self-certification

> **Our Lovely System cannot be the sole judge that Our Lovely System has been proved.**

The component under test may generate evidence. It may not be the sole authority declaring that the evidence is sufficient, the test was fair, or the system has earned trust.

Verification may come from users, independent reproduction, contradictory evidence, receipts, public consequence, hostile review, or other declared constituencies.

## Relationship to voting

[RFC 0001](./0001-capital-disposition-voting-test.md) is a proof feature.

A displayed tally proves only that an interface displayed a tally. The proposed test places capital under a published vote, executes the winning disposition, and publishes evidence connecting result to transfer.

The signal is not “Pledge supports voting.” The signal is the capital arriving where the authorized vote directed.

## Relationship to Ours to Give

[RFC 0003](./0003-ours-to-give.md) creates a market in proposed offerings and human judgment.

An OTG writer proposes value. Other users may acknowledge, accept, and honor the pledge. The differences among offered, acknowledged, accepted, and honored balances are market responses to the artifacts and people associated with them.

An honored OTG is evidence that someone carried a proposed offering into consequence. If the resulting capital enters the voting test, the complete proof chain may connect:

> offering → acknowledgment → acceptance → honoring → vote → disposition → evidence

## Response to hostile review

The hostile review is sustained in substantial part.

Proof being a market signal does not mean markets reliably discover truth, virtue, or justice. Markets can reward:

- spectacle over substance;
- repeated claims over stronger evidence;
- signals affordable only to powerful participants;
- coercive participation;
- selective publication;
- legibility to insiders rather than affected people;
- and evidence optimized for the metric used to judge it.

Therefore:

1. Market response must not be presented as moral ratification.
2. Participation must not automatically be interpreted as trust.
3. Silence and exit must not be assigned invented motives.
4. The cost and beneficiary of each proof must be visible.
5. Hostile reproduction and contradiction must remain possible.
6. Proof conditions and omitted populations must be disclosed.
7. Different constituencies should remain separately visible when their responses differ.
8. Pledge must not optimize itself toward one proof score and call the result virtue.

The review does not defeat the premise. It identifies what makes the signal interpretable rather than merely loud.

## Failure modes

The proof model fails when:

- the claim is broadened after the test succeeds;
- the conditions are hidden or changed;
- the test has no real consequence;
- the executing component certifies itself without external challenge;
- only favorable results are published;
- participation is presented as trust or consent;
- silence is converted into agreement;
- market popularity is converted into moral authority;
- evidence exposes vulnerable people more than powerful actors;
- a costly signal is treated as inherently truthful; or
- the proof record becomes too complex for an affected person to reconstruct.

## Functional requirements for proof features

A Pledge feature whose purpose is to prove a claim should:

1. publish the bounded claim before the test;
2. publish the governing rule and version;
3. identify what real consequence distinguishes the test from demonstration theater;
4. preserve success, failure, dissent, and correction;
5. expose the cost, beneficiary, and bearer of consequence;
6. prevent the tested component from serving as sole verifier;
7. state what remains unproved;
8. preserve constituency differences in market response;
9. protect vulnerable participants from unnecessary exposure; and
10. leave evidence an independent reader can reconstruct.

## Acceptance criteria

This RFC may be considered successfully implemented in a feature when an independent reader can determine:

1. what claim was tested;
2. under what conditions;
3. what was placed at risk;
4. what consequence occurred;
5. what evidence remains;
6. who verified or challenged it;
7. how people responded;
8. which responses were not observable;
9. what value was added or subtracted; and
10. what the test did not prove.

## Open questions

- Which constituencies may verify different kinds of proof?
- How should Pledge represent conflicting market signals?
- When does the cost of a proof exceed its evidentiary value?
- How should refusal and exit be recorded without inventing motive?
- What constitutes independent verification when users and components overlap?
- How should a proof be retired when environmental change makes it obsolete?
- Which proof features should exist solely to demonstrate that voting, receipting, deletion, or correction works?

## Disposition

This RFC is proposed. It does not declare Our Lovely System proved, establish a universal proof score, or convert market response into moral authority.
